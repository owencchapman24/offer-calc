## Summary

**What's in good shape:**
- Every check passes: 485 unit tests, the typecheck, the engine guard, the build and bundle budget, 164 Playwright tests with no
  accessibility violations, and Lighthouse at LCP 1.88 s.
- `npm audit` is clean, the live headers match `vercel.json`, and the git history holds nothing that shouldn't be public.

**Where the risks are:** just outside the three sample offers, which file single, start on the same day, live outside

- Philadelphia's wage tax is never applied, although the methodology says it is.

**Docs:** CLAUDE.md (87 KB) has drifted from the code in a dozen places, including the oracle baseline that tax changes are checked
against. It should shrink by about half.

**Headroom:** 10.2 kB of bundle and about 0.6 s of LCP. Neither the worker's size nor the time to the first verdict is measured.

## Top five findings

1. **Married filers are taxed as though their spouse earns nothing ([C1](#c1-married-filers-are-taxed-as-though-their-spouse-earns-nothing)).**
   - There's no spouse-wages field.
   - The help says joint filing "adds your spouse's wages", and the engine supports them.
   - For a joint filer with a working spouse, each offer's tax is understated, typically by thousands a year.
   - Fix before v2.
2. **Philadelphia's wage tax is never applied ([C2](#c2-philadelphias-wage-tax-is-never-applied-though-the-methodology-says-it-is)).**
   - The methodology says it's included, but the app only ever sets New York City as a locality.
   - A Philadelphia resident is under-taxed by about $3,700 per $100,000 of pay, every year.
   - Fix before v2.
3. **The comparison window shortchanges an offer that starts later ([C3](#c3-a-later-starting-offer-loses-its-last-partial-month-of-pay-in-the-comparison), with C4 and C5).**
   - Pay accrued in the window's last month is dated at month end, outside the window. A later-starting offer loses up to a month
     of pay while its hours for those days still count: $3,871 in a 21-day example.
   - Real comparisons rarely share a start date.
   - Two more timing bugs change the verdict or L-2: a cash award paid before the start date, and a guaranteed first bonus after
     a cutoff.
   - Fix before v2.
4. **The verdict's "Show the work" doesn't add up as displayed ([C12](#c12-the-verdicts-show-the-work-doesnt-add-up-as-displayed-and-one-gap-shows-as-two-numbers) to C15).**
   - Present-value derivations show unrounded gaps under rounded figures.
   - The same gap reads 21,156 in the hero and 21,157 in L-3 and the copied document.
   - The verdict's "a month" is rounded twice, so it can say $488 where L-2 says $487.
   - The tests allow ±$2 there, and only on the samples.
   - Fix before v2.
5. **Failures have no way back ([C22](#c22-a-malformed-saved-session-blanks-the-app-on-every-visit) to C24).**
   - A malformed saved session crashes every page, including the Data page that would delete it.
   - A session saved by a newer schema is silently replaced by the samples, and v2 plans schema 4.
   - A chunk missing after a deploy, or a worker that won't load, leaves the page empty or "Updating…" forever.
   - There's no error boundary anywhere.
   - Fix before v2 (C22, C23) and before going public (C24).

#### C1. Married filers are taxed as though their spouse earns nothing
**Fix before v2.** Verified.
- **The inputs:**
  - About you offers "Married, filing jointly" (`app/src/About.tsx:14`).
  - `toHousehold` (`app/src/model.ts:503-510`) never sets `spouseWages`.
  - The engine supports them (`src/offer/types.ts:231`; `src/offer/project.ts:166`, `spouseWages?.[year] ?? 0`).
- **What the docs say:**
  - The filing-status help: "Married filing jointly adds your spouse's wages, if any" (`app/src/quickHelp.ts:33-34`).
  - M-10: "Spouse wages are the same for every offer" (`Methodology.tsx:432`).
  - CLAUDE.md:954.
  - L-6's spouse row (`workpaper.ts:445`) can never appear.
- **Why it matters:** joint brackets are applied to the offer alone. With a working spouse, the offer's pay really stacks on the
  spouse's: often in the 22% joint bracket rather than 12%, with state tax likewise.
  - After-tax figures are overstated by thousands a year.
  - The gap between offers is distorted where they straddle brackets.
- **Fix:**
  - Add "Your spouse's wages" to About you when filing jointly (one figure, grown with inflation, is enough).
  - Until then, say beside the choice that spouse wages are taken as $0, and correct the help, M-10 and CLAUDE.md.
  - Test joint filing in the app (T4).

#### C3. A later-starting offer loses its last partial month of pay in the comparison
**Fix before v2.** Verified (reproduced).
- **Where:**
  - An event is in the window by its pay date (`src/offer/project.ts:247`).
  - Accrued pay is cut at month starts and at the offer's own anniversaries, never at the window's end: salary at
    `src/offer/streams.ts:96-97`, benefits at `src/offer/benefits.ts:113`, rent at `project.ts:380-383`.
  - Hours are prorated by day up to the window's end (`project.ts:420-428`).
- **Evidence:** both offers TX, $120,000, 40 hours a week, a 4-year window from 2026-07-13.

  | | Starts | Pay in the window | Hours | Per hour |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | A | 2026-07-13 | $480,000 | | $47.31 |
  | B | 2026-08-03 | $469,354.84 | 7,884.93 | $46.93 |

  - B's July 2030 pay is one piece dated Jul 31, outside the window, though Jul 1-12 are inside.
  - B's pay for its own days in the window should be $473,225.81, so B loses an extra $3,870.97.
  - Its hours count those days, which is why its per hour falls below A's at the same pay rate.
- **Why it matters:**

### P1. Bundle headroom is 10.2 kB, and the stylesheet and the worker aren't budgeted

### P2. LCP has about 0.6 s of headroom and measures the page before the answer
**Fix before v2.** Verified.

  - Time to the first verdict isn't measured. v2's plan item 4 adds a `performance.mark`; it isn't built.

  - One CI run spread 0.46 s across its three measurements, so a 0.3 s regression plus a slow run can fail CI on noise.
  - Meanwhile a verdict two seconds slower would pass.
- **Fix:**
  - Build v2-plan's item 4 (mark the first verdict's paint, read it from the trace, report it and later gate it) before v2's first
    feature.
  - Consider five runs in CI.

### P3. CLS and TBT aren't gated

### P4. The one-chunk rule isn't enforced, and a comment says it is
**Fix before v2.** Verified.
- **The rule:** CLAUDE.md says a split costs 1-2 kB gzip on every visit, and that `grep -c modulepreload dist/index.html` is 0

### P5. Dimmed figures fall to 2.99:1 contrast, and the scan is built never to see them
**Fix before going public: needs a decision.** Verified.
- **The dimming:** the lead and every schedule dim to opacity 0.6 while updating (`app/src/styles.css:189`, `:681`, `:701-702`,
  `:737`, `:786`, `:834`).
- **What axe found:** in CI run 38017599681, axe caught L-4 dimmed during another state's scan. #8f9591 on #fcfdfc is 2.99:1,
  against the 4.5:1 required.
- **How the scan avoids it:** `quiet()` waits for the dimming to end, by design.
- **The asymmetry:** CLAUDE.md gives "⚠ Last valid results" full contrast for WCAG 1.4.3, but not "Updating…".
- **How long it lasts:**
  - the lead dims for about 250 ms on each keystroke;
  - L-4 for 0.7-1 s on a desktop, and longer on a slow phone.
- **Why it matters:** WCAG 1.4.3 has no exemption for content being updated. A public accessibility claim should meet it or state
  the exception.
- **Fix:** decide one of:
  - keep text contrast, and show the update another way (a band, a hatch, or the "Updating…" label alone);
  - or add a reasoned entry to `test/a11y-exceptions.json`, plus one scan of the dimmed state, so the decision is visible.
